# Limitations and roadmap

## What works

- CSV import, rule-based classification, exposure statistics, pool classification, Black-76 pricing, cohort backtest, scenario arithmetic and policy replay. 40 unit tests: 31 for the engine, 9 for the ledger import and sign-in mapping.
- Live Gemini Prediction Markets data: discovery, suitability screens, implied distribution and ladder replication.
- Ledger sign-in code for QuickBooks Online, Xero, Zoho Books and FreshBooks (`lib/ledger/`, `/api/connect/*`). It is unit-tested against mocked provider responses: QuickBooks end to end (token, company and transaction list), the other three for line mapping only. It has not been verified against a live provider account. The demo has no provider keys, so the Books screen shows one line saying sign-in is built but switched off. CSV upload is the working path.
- A settlement program on Solana devnet with Helius Privacy Rings, and a server flow that opens a policy as a ladder of steps, funds the steps privately, posts the oracle report, releases triggered steps to the fleet, and shows the refused attempts. A lender page decrypts the steps with read-only viewing keys.
- Earlier work, archived and not the settlement path: a fully collateralised USDC vault written for Arc testnet with 15 Foundry tests (`contracts/`). It was not deployed.

## What is simulated

- **The sample company.** Synthetic. Fuel prices paid are built from the real EIA Midwest index plus seeded noise, so its R² to the index reflects the generator, not a real fleet.
- **The premium.** A model price on EIA spot with an assumed load. No live options quotes, no skew, no bid/offer. No premium is collected, on chain or off: the program has no premium instruction and the fleet pays nothing.
- **The oracle.** One trusted key posting public history.
- **The policy.** Nobody sold it and nobody owes it. The onchain policy funds one month's steps in tUSD, devnet test dollars at 1 tUSD = $1.
- **The fleet wallet.** A demo wallet the server controls, so devnet funds can be recycled.

## Solana and privacy limitations

- **Custodial demo.** The server holds the fleet, underwriter and oracle keys and every step's viewing key, and signs for every party. In production each party would sign in its own wallet.
- **Lender decryption runs on our server.** The lender link carries the viewing keys in the URL fragment, which browsers do not send with page requests. The `/lender` page then posts them to the Covenant server, which decrypts. A production version would decrypt in the lender's browser.
- **Helius's prover sees plaintext.** Privacy Rings proofs are generated by Helius inside attested hardware (a TEE), which sees amounts in the clear.
- **Funding is not verified on chain.** Amounts are encrypted, so the program cannot check that a step's vault holds the right amount. The fleet or its lender checks by decrypting.
- **Release recipient is not enforced.** The program checks the trigger and that the fleet signed, but cannot see inside the encrypted transfer. Once a step is triggered, the fleet's release can route the funds anywhere. The same holds for the underwriter's reclaim.
- **Terms are public.** Trigger prices, the number of steps, and which steps paid are visible. Anyone who knew the step size and the fleet's covered gallons could infer amounts. The gallons are not published.
- **Public onramp.** Depositing tUSD into a private balance reveals the deposit amount.
- **Steps, not a linear cap.** The program cannot compute `(index - strike) x gallons` on encrypted amounts, so it pays in $0.25-per-gallon steps up to $2.00 above the strike. A month just below the next trigger pays up to $0.25 a gallon less than the linear cap. Larger steps ($0.50) would be used if transaction size limits force fewer steps.
- **Alpha software.** The Privacy Rings SDK (`@heliuslabs/spr`) is an alpha, and its program-owned balance helpers are marked experimental.
- **Explorer support.** Privacy Rings uses v1 transactions. Some explorers and RPC clients cannot render them yet.
- **Write-once reports are global.** One report per index and month. A rerun of the demo reuses the report already on chain and says so.
- **Upgradeable program.** One team wallet, the one that deployed the program (`84ZgVXHPfpYML8E3o63aLqMo69Z4A6o4cxUY4r7xuWRE`), holds the upgrade authority and can replace the code.
- **Unfinished runs stay open.** Nothing on chain expires. A settlement run that stops midway leaves its remaining steps open and funded, and releasing or reclaiming them needs each step's vault key, which only the server that ran it keeps. One devnet policy (`2c7cb8f46c1f57c2`) had 3 steps paid and 5 open when read on Nov 20. There is no recovery tool.
- **No settlement without the team's keys.** A server that lacks them, including the hosted demo, shows the replay and no settle button. For the sample fleet the payout screen says "This settlement ran on Solana devnet" and links the team's Nov 20 devnet transactions; it does not send a new payment. The amount it shows in 25-cent steps is worked out from the public price and the fleet's gallons, because the on-chain amounts are encrypted. Settlement has run only from a team machine; the README lists the devnet transactions.

## What was not built

- An LLM fallback for unmatched expense lines.
- More than one priced risk. Natural gas, electricity and interest are sized and screened, not priced.
- Regions other than the Midwest and the national average.
- Any real risk carrier, reinsurer, options execution or prediction-market order.
- Per-user accounts and persistence.
- Self-custody wallets for the fleet, the underwriter or the lender.

## Known weaknesses

- The un-surcharged share is inferred from revenue mix, which is a proxy for gallons.
- Shock percentiles come from 25 annual observations.
- The implied volatility comes from Brent, not ULSD, at a horizon of days, with quote spreads of several cents.
- Order-book depth is read only for the chosen ladder, so every other Gemini market shows a depth of 0.
- Gemini fees and collateral rules were not verified and are left out of the replication cost.
- The program trusts a single oracle key. It stores the data file's sha256 but cannot check it against EIA.
- Pricing and the replay use the linear cap; on chain the cap pays in steps, which is slightly less.
- The recovery-in-bad-periods figure differs from the research because the research did not define "bad period" precisely.

## What production would need

1. A licensed insurer or surplus-lines carrier to owe the payouts, and counsel's view on whether purchase-linked price cover is insurance or a swap in each state.
2. A reinsurer for the retail-to-wholesale basis and a broker for pooled ULSD options.
3. Fuel-card integration, so gallons are measured rather than read from memos.
4. A real oracle: signed EIA data from more than one publisher, with a dispute window.
5. Wallets where each party holds its own keys, and lender decryption in the lender's own browser.
6. Evidence that owners will pay. Index insurance take-up at market prices is usually low, and buyers who arrive after a price spike are adversely selected.
