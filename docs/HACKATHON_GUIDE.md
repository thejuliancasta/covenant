## What you enter

One project enters several prize pools at once.

1. **One main track (required).**
   - Agentic Payments and AI: agents and infrastructure that safely hold, send and receive stablecoins.
   - **DeFi and Onchain Applications: new financial primitives and smart-contract applications. This is ours.**
   - Consumer Applications: everyday apps where the crypto is invisible.
2. **At most one chain award (optional).** Best use of Solana or Best use of Arc, never both. Judged within your track. You must build on the chain; devnet or testnet is fine, and using existing deployed programs counts. **Ours is Solana.**
3. **Any number of sponsor bounties (optional).** Each has its own technical requirements.
4. **The overall prize.** Every submission is eligible automatically.

## Submission checklist

From the Devpost page:

- [ ] Project name, description, all team members
- [ ] Main track, chain award and bounties selected on the form, with opt-ins
- [ ] Written answers: What did you build? What problem does it solve? How does it work? What makes it unique? What would you build next? (drafts in `docs/SUBMISSION.md`)
- [ ] Technologies and sponsor tools used
- [ ] Public GitHub repository created during the hackathon
- [ ] Working demo or deployed application
- [ ] 2 to 3 minute demo video on YouTube or Google Drive, accessible to judges
- [ ] README with technical documentation and an architecture overview
- [ ] Track-specific requirements, such as contract or program addresses

DeFi track extra: economic design and risk assumptions in the README, plus program addresses **[guide]**.

## Rules that can disqualify

- **All code must be created during the event.** Pre-existing projects and old codebases are not allowed.
- **You must be present in person at the final presentations to receive any prize**, bounties included **[rules page; "bounties included" is from the guide]**.
- Teams are 1 to 4 people. Solo entries are allowed.
- AI tools are allowed, but the builders must remain the primary creators.
- Participants keep ownership of their work.

## Judging

Five criteria, no published weights:

1. Technical execution: functionality, depth, effective use of blockchain infrastructure
2. Product and UX: clear problem, intuitive experience, quality
3. Innovation: originality
4. Impact and potential: real-world usefulness and adoption potential
5. Demo: quality of the live demonstration and communication

How it runs:

- Up to 13 finalists: three per main track (track winner, best Solana and best Arc contenders), the top three overall contenders, and the first-place Gemini bounty team.
- Finalists pitch live. The pitch is 5 minutes plus 2 minutes of questions **[guide]**.
- Bounty winners are chosen from the written submission by the sponsor and a UBC representative **[guide]**. So the video and README decide most outcomes.
- Judges come from the Solana, Circle, ElevenLabs, Gemini, QuickNode and Cursor teams.

## Places where the official pages disagree

Check with the organisers if any of these matter to a decision.

- **Start date.** The hacking window starts Nov 12 at 6:00 PM, but the requirements say the project must be built "entirely during the hackathon week (Nov 13 – Nov 20)" and the rules page says work before Nov 13 is not allowed.
- **Eligibility.** The Devpost overview says students only and UBC participants only. The rules page says students, independent builders and community members.
- **Where finalists pitch.** One section says the closing ceremony, another says Saturday Nov 21. Both may be true.
